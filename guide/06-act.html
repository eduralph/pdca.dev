Step 06 — Act: improve the process

← 05 Check · Index · next: 07 Cross-cutting →

Beat: Act. Touch point #3, and the last one. Plan/Do/Check operate on one contribution. Act operates on the process itself, across many completed cycles, on its own cadence (every N cycles, or weekly — not per-cycle). It's what closes the PDCA loop: turning what several cycles exposed into a permanently better baseline, so the next Plan starts from firmer ground.

Two parts follow: how to use it — the commands, and three real process deltas it produced — then how it works underneath: the leaf, what it reads (and explicitly refuses to do), where a delta can land, and the cadence that keeps it from firing on noise.

How to use

pdca act index                              # read-only: frozen cycles + recurring signals
pdca act log --date 2026-10-14 --append     # scaffold a dated entry into process/act-log.md
pdca triage <pr>                            # ingest a published PR's external review findings

Act reads the COMPLETE bundles' SUMMARYs — especially §6 (what needed a human), §7 (what stayed unproven), and §10 (Act candidates the cycle flagged) — looking for patterns. A one-off is noise; the same gap across three cycles is a process defect.

A real Act entry

This is from wyrd-pdca/process/act-log.md (2026-09-20), and it's the clearest demonstration of why the beat exists. A post-merge review of Wyrd PR #821 — bundle 800, a garbage-collection change rated medium — found three P1 defects the cycle had not surfaced. The review asked why the cycle missed them, not just what they were (trimmed; the full entry is about sixty lines):

# Act review — 2026-09-20 — review coverage after wyrd PR #821 (no new frozen cycles; decided by Eduard Ralph in session)

## What the records exposed

- **The refutation pass never ran on #800.** `[[leaves.advisory]] adversary` was gated on
  `difficulty` containing "high"/"hard"; #800 and its parent #661 were rated `medium`, so the
  driver skipped it silently — no artifact, no note in §5. Every bundle that got an adversary
  pass (#771, #772, #803, #804, #635, #636, #681, #697) was rated `high`; #661, #693, #800
  (`medium`) and #655 (`low`) got none. ... `difficulty` is a size label the planner writes, not
  a statement about which invariants a slice touches.
- **Two findings were in nobody's pass.** The batched pre-PR review (3 codex passes) returned
  0 findings on a 119 KB patch the size backstop had flagged. Its rubric has no row for a
  transaction's sequential operation count (only bytes) ... Both are the
  correctness lens `agents/code-review.md` describes, which no leaf ran.
- **The ladder topped out on one lineage.** Escalation went sonnet/high → opus/max and stayed
  on opus; #637 hit an Opus-specific refusal twice in a row with no other family to fall to.

## Process deltas

- Gates / leaves: **three-tier builder ladder by attempt** — tier 1 sonnet/xhigh (every first
  attempt), tier 2 opus/xhigh (attempts 2 to 4), tier 3 fable/xhigh (attempt 5 on). ...
  The difficulty auto-route to opus is retired; `Do model:` pins remain ...
- Gates / leaves: **adversary always on** — the `when` gate on `difficulty` is removed.
  (`pdca.toml` `[[leaves.advisory]] id = "adversary"`)
- Gates / leaves: **third-party code-review lens always on** — new `[[leaves.advisory]]
  id = "code-review"`, codex family, `agents/code-review.md`, advisory.
- Spec template + agent prompt: **`Difficulty` no longer routes anything at Do or Check.**
  The planner prompt and the three brief templates now say so, name what the field still
  feeds (the size estimate), and tell the planner to pin `Do model:` ... for a brief it
  rates `high`, since the rating alone no longer buys a stronger first build.

## How effectiveness will be judged

- Every Check from here on shows three advisory artifacts (`check-review.md`,
  `check-advisory-adversary.md`, `check-advisory-code-review.md`); a missing one is a fault,
  not a gate.
- The next post-merge review on a merged PR finds nothing the cycle did not already have in
  §6. Two in a row that do trigger a rubric revision, not another leaf.

Trace the loop: three defects found after merge (§ outside the cycle) → Act generalizes them ("the refutation pass was keyed on a field that measures size, not risk") → concrete process deltas (two review leaves always on, a ladder that climbs by attempt instead of by self-rating, a template that says so) → which means every later cycle's Check runs the lens that would have caught them. The 779 bundle in step 05 shows the result: check-advisory-adversary.md and check-advisory-code-review.md beside the reviewer's report on a medium bundle, exactly as the entry's first effectiveness test demands. That is the "→ back to Plan with a better baseline" arrow made operational. Note the last line, too: the entry pre-commits to what would prove the delta wrong, and names the different fix that would follow.

The 779 findings, one review later

The two harness-shaped §6 items from step 05 — the diff-coverage row that gave the wrong reason, and the three-pass review that finished in 26 seconds with nothing to say — are precisely the kind of signal Act exists for: not a defect in the patch, a defect in the instrument. The 2026-10-14 review read 779 and nine other frozen cycles and found both were patterns, not one-offs (trimmed):

Three things to notice. The adversary's §6 finding on 779 ("the coverage checkout is on a base without #778") was right, and Act found the cause one level deeper: a config key copied from guidance that was itself misleading — so one follow-up fixes the instance and another fixes the template's words, each filed where it belongs. The thin batch review turned out to be two code defects in an instance script, filed as an issue with a test requirement rather than patched in the review session. And the third delta, applied on the spot, is a vocabulary fix: four planners had named the same base-wide dependency failure four different ways, so the agent prompts now carry one standing id for it.

Act also fixes gates

An earlier entry shows Act tightening a gate, not a leaf. On 2026-08-02 a review of eighteen frozen cycles found that the instance's own per-fix verifier had a hole in its red leg (trimmed):

- **Gates — the RED leg's non-zero path is guarded (the 07-25 queued delta, now applied).**
  A pure `_red_verdict()` decides all four cells from cargo's status **and** what ran
  (`engine/scripts/run-verify.sh:181`), the RED leg routes through it (`:478-507`), and the
  `≠0 status / 0 tests` cell now reports **UNVERIFIABLE (exit 77 → §6 NEEDS-HUMAN)** with a
  message naming the usual cause, instead of PASS. ...
  *Known cost, accepted:* a slice whose RED leg genuinely cannot compile now raises a §6 item
  instead of passing silently — visible rather than free.

A compile error exits non-zero just like a failing assertion does, so a red leg judged on the exit code alone had been recording "PASS — red without the fix" for tests that never built. The delta judges every leg by two facts — the exit code and the number of tests that ran — and the template's engine skeleton now carries the same rule, routed upstream as issue #434 from this entry. That is why 779's C4-verify row says "(1 test(s) ran red)".

Closing the loop — the process-delta ledger

A delta is only worth anything if it works. Act keeps a process/act-ledger.json: each recurring signal it surfaces is tracked open; once you land the fix you run

pdca act resolve "<signal>" --location <path:line>

to mark it applied; and on a later review Act flags any applied delta whose miss recurs in a cycle frozen after the applied date — a loud "⚠ Ineffective deltas" section in act index / the act log scaffold. So Act audits its own prescriptions instead of writing them and forgetting. The "How effectiveness will be judged" section every Wyrd entry ends with is the human half of the same habit.

External review findings — pdca triage (issue #316)

The maintainer review on a published PR is the outer loop's highest-value signal — and, until someone transcribes it, invisible to Act. pdca triage does the transcription:

pdca triage https://github.com/OWNER/REPO/pull/123   # or OWNER/REPO#123, or a bare number with --repo

It pulls the PR's reviews and review comments via gh api, classifies each finding (BUG / CONVENTION / NOISE / TEST-GAP — keyword heuristics, tunable from the instance rubric's class list), and routes by class: a BUG on a merged PR files a tracker issue whose body carries a carry-forward note; CONVENTION / NOISE / TEST-GAP append candidate gate-row, rubric-line and rubric-exclusion entries to process/act-log.md. Every finding also registers in the Act ledger under a class-keyed signal (codex-pr:<slug>), so the ineffective-delta audit above covers external findings too: a class that recurs after its process delta was applied gets flagged. It proposes only — it never edits pdca.toml or the rubric — and re-running the same PR ingests only findings that arrived since the last run. Wyrd's INTEGRATION §8 makes it part of the definition of done for a PR: when external findings arrive, process them with pdca triage so every one lands in the ledger.

A spec-level delta can also become a mechanical one. The template ships a reference check, scripts/checks/test_exercises_production.py (a [[gates.checks]] example, issue #154), which asserts each added test file imports the production package and declares itself UNVERIFIABLE → §6 when it can't — promoting "test the production path, not a parallel copy" from a human-only T5 judgment to a deterministically-surfaced one. Wyrd's briefs carry the same rule in their Production reach field.

How it works

The leaf Act uses

Act has one leaf — act, interactive, like planner and signoff: the human touch point is the whole beat, not a step inside it. The leaf instruments the process — presenting the bundle index and recurring-signal candidates — but pdca act index's pattern-detection and the ledger are pure code; the leaf only helps you draft the dated entry.

What Act reads, and what it explicitly does not do

Act reads COMPLETE bundles only — frozen records, never anything in-flight. What it explicitly does not do: re-decide any disposition, re-run the suite, or write the next brief. It generalizes a pattern into a candidate delta; a human still judges what to actually change (what rule to add, which template field to revise, whether to retire a check) — the bundle index surfaces patterns, the human decides. Wyrd's recent entries open with that disclaimer ("No disposition is re-decided") and the 2026-10-19 review shows the discipline holding under temptation: a one-line config fix for the diff-coverage base was offered in session and declined — "fixes stay with their issues, this review only files."

Where deltas land

Act deltas land in five places: the spec template (a brief field), the ruleset (a written rule), the gates (a new/promoted check), the agent role prompts (agents/*.md), or the orchestration (driver/state). Each is a permanent improvement to the baseline every future cycle starts from — the next Do should not recreate the issue the previous Act tried to eliminate; if it does, the Act wasn't effective (which is exactly what the ledger's "⚠ Ineffective deltas" check is for). The three entries above hit four of the five: leaves and gates (2026-09-20, 2026-08-02), the spec template (2026-09-20), the agent prompts (2026-10-14).

Cadence — why Act doesn't fire every cycle

[driver].act_cadence (default 5) is why "every N cycles, not per-cycle" is a real mechanism, not just a suggestion: pdca flow auto-runs Act only once this many cycles have frozen since the last Act review, counted across flow invocations (five separate one-bundle flows trip it on the fifth, same as one five-bundle batch would). Below the threshold, a flow skips Act with a hint instead of running it on too little evidence to show a pattern; pdca act log still runs it on demand any time, and --no-act always forces a skip. Set act_cadence = 1 to restore "run after every completed flow." Wyrd runs the default, and its log shows the other kind of entry too: an out-of-band review, triggered by an event rather than the count — the post-merge findings of 2026-09-20 were one.

The loop, closed

You've now walked one contribution from a tracker issue to a merged, signed-off, verified change — and seen how the cycle feeds its own improvement. One more thing worth knowing before you go: three mechanisms — sizing/splitting an oversized brief, how an iterate actually archives and carries context forward, and running several cycles concurrently — cut across every beat above rather than belonging to one of them. Step 07 covers them.
